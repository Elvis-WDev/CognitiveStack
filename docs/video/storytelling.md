# Storytelling

A problem video succeeds when the viewer thinks "that is my Friday night" before they
think anything about a product. Narrate the pain they live, in the place they live it, with
the cost they already feel.

## Principles

- **One problem per video.** Two problems split attention and weaken both.
- **Show, do not tell.** A pile of paper tickets sliding off the pass says more than "orders
  get lost". Text names what the image already shows; it does not replace it.
- **Specific beats abstract.** "Table 12 waited 25 minutes for a lost order" beats
  "inefficient operations". Use a concrete table, dish, time, and number.
- **The system is the villain, never the people.** Staff are overloaded, not incompetent.
  Never mock a waiter, cook, or customer; the viewer is one of them.
- **Recognition before resolution.** Spend most of the time on the problem. A solution that
  arrives before the viewer feels the pain is an ad they skip.
- **Escalate, do not repeat.** Each beat makes the problem worse or more expensive. Two beats
  that show the same pain at the same intensity waste time.
- **Every number is defensible.** Use sourced figures, or present a number explicitly as an
  example. Never invent a statistic, a customer, or a result.
- **Muted first.** Most viewers watch without sound. The image and the on-screen text carry
  the story; voice and music reinforce it.

## Structure

Six beats. Each beat has one job and one focal point.

| Beat       | Job                                                        | Example (lost orders)                                   |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | Show the moment of pain in the first frames                | A ticket slips off the pass and drifts under the counter |
| Context    | Place the viewer: who, where, when                         | Full dining room, 9 p.m., one waiter, eight tables      |
| Escalation | Make the problem compound in two or three beats            | Tickets pile up, the kitchen cooks the wrong dish, a table waits |
| Cost       | Make the consequence concrete and countable                | Wait time counter climbs, a one-star review appears     |
| Turn       | A brief stillness that resets attention                    | Everything freezes; one line asks the question          |
| Resolution | Show how the same scene works when the problem is solved   | Orders flow from table to kitchen in order, on time     |
| Close      | Land the message and the action                            | Message line, logo or call to action, held long enough to read |

### Timing

| Beat       | 15 s        | 30 s        | 60 s        |
| ---------- | ----------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| Hook       | 0.0-2.0     | 0.0-3.0     | 0.0-4.0     |
| Context    | merged      | 3.0-7.0     | 4.0-12.0    |
| Escalation | 2.0-8.0     | 7.0-17.0    | 12.0-32.0   |
| Cost       | 8.0-10.0    | 17.0-21.0   | 32.0-40.0   |
| Turn       | 10.0-10.8   | 21.0-22.5   | 40.0-42.0   |
| Resolution | 10.8-13.5   | 22.5-27.5   | 42.0-55.0   |
| Close      | 13.5-15.0   | 27.5-30.0   | 55.0-60.0   |

- The first frame is already a finished composition, never an empty canvas waiting for
  something to fly in.
- The problem is visible within two seconds.
- The close holds still for at least 1.5 seconds so the message can be read.

## Motion Tells The Story

Use the quality of motion itself as narrative, not only the content:

| Problem phase                         | Resolution phase                                |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Desynchronized, irregular timing      | Synchronized to one rhythm                      |
| Objects off the grid, piling, sliding | Objects snapped to the grid, aligned, ordered   |
| Many small competing loops            | Few calm loops, one clear flow                  |
| Problem accent color spreading        | Problem accent gone, solution accent appears once |
| Faster tempo as costs grow            | Slower, steady tempo                            |

The turn between them is stillness: 0.4 to 0.8 seconds where nothing moves. The contrast
makes the resolution feel earned. See `motion.md`.

## Restaurant Problem Catalog

Starting points, not scripts. Each one needs its own brief.

| Problem                         | Visual metaphor                                           | Micro-animations                                        | Cost to show                          |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Lost or wrong orders            | Paper tickets between dining room and kitchen             | Ticket flutter, printer stutter, bell ringing unanswered | Wait time, remade dishes, bad review  |
| No-shows                        | Reserved tables set and empty while a queue waits outside | Clock hand, candle flame, people shifting in line       | Empty covers, lost revenue per night  |
| Rush hour bottleneck            | One till, a long queue folding around the room            | Queue shuffle, card reader blinking, door swinging      | Walk-outs, time per order             |
| Too many delivery tablets       | Four tablets on the counter, each a different color       | Tablets vibrating and lighting out of sync              | Missed orders, staff tied to the counter |
| Food waste                      | Cold room shelves emptying into the bin at close          | Lid opening, items sliding, scale needle moving         | Kilos and money thrown away weekly    |
| Stock outs mid-service          | An empty shelf where the best-selling ingredient should be | Hand reaching into empty space, dish crossed off menu  | Dishes unavailable, disappointed tables |
| Staff scheduling chaos          | A paper rota with crossed-out names and sticky notes      | Notes peeling, phone buzzing with swap requests         | Short-staffed shifts, overtime        |
| Slow table turnover             | Finished tables waiting for the bill                      | Raised hand, empty glasses, clock                       | Covers lost per service               |
| End-of-day cash reconciliation  | A manager alone at closing, receipts and calculator       | Lamp hum, receipts curling, numbers not matching        | Hours after close, errors             |

## Storyboard Format

Write the storyboard before any animation, as one table:

| Time      | Beat       | Scene and focal point | Motion            | On-screen text   | Sound          | Transition out        |
| --------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------- | -------------- | --------------------- |
| 0.0-3.0   | Hook       | The pass; one ticket  | Ticket slips off  | "9:14 p.m."      | Kitchen ambience | Camera follows ticket |

Every row has exactly one focal point, and every transition names the object or shape that
carries the viewer into the next row.

## Verification

- [ ] The video brief is answered in writing.
- [ ] One problem, one message, one action.
- [ ] The problem is visible within two seconds, and the first frame is a finished composition.
- [ ] Every beat escalates; none repeats the previous one.
- [ ] No person is mocked; the system is the source of the problem.
- [ ] Every number is sourced or explicitly framed as an example.
- [ ] The story reads with the sound off.
- [ ] The storyboard has one focal point per row and a named transition out of each scene.
